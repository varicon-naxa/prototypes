The invoice asks for exactly the retention more than the claim says is owed. It is not a rounding error or a rate mismatch — the deduction line is simply never written onto the invoice, so retention accrues on the claim and stays in the amount billed. The contractor is asking their client for money the contract says the client keeps.

It fails in the direction that gets noticed by the person paying, not the person sending. Every one of these is a client of our client querying an invoice.

Why it has not been caught more often

Three things have to line up before anyone sees it, which is why this reads as rare and is not.
1
The claim screen is right. Anyone checking the numbers in Varicon sees the correct $312,000 and has no reason to open the invoice.
2
The invoice is arithmetically consistent with itself. $340,000 plus GST is $374,000. Nothing in Xero flags it — it is only wrong against a document Xero never sees.
3
It only bites on retention-bearing contracts. On everything else the gross and the net are the same number, and the missing line changes nothing.
VDP-2282 Write the deduction line onto the invoice. Then make it a setting, because a minority of contracts genuinely bill gross — the client withholds retention at their end. Default on. Flip the toggle below and the invoice redraws.

The position is stamped on the claim, not read from settings

Recorded when the invoice is raised. Changing the setting later must never alter an invoice already sent.
This is the part that is easy to get wrong. If the invoice renders by reading the current setting, then turning the toggle off next month silently rewrites what INV-1184 says — and it will no longer match the PDF the client is holding. The claim has to carry its own answer.

What this fix does not decide

Dependency GST. This prototype puts GST on the full claim — the supply is the whole $340,000 and retention is money withheld from payment, not a reduction in what was supplied. That is the treatment that keeps the invoice total at $346,000 and matches the claim. It is a read, not advice, and it needs confirming with an accountant and against what Xero does on the ACCREC before this is specced. If GST instead sits on the net, the invoice is $343,200 and every corrected figure moves.
Sequencing The toggle must not ship ahead of the deduction line. Until invoices deduct by default there is no correct default to build a setting against, and gross invoicing stops being a bug and becomes a setting nobody chose. Fix first, then expose the exception.
A fix that only changes future behaviour leaves the wrong invoices where they are. Some are paid. We do not need to wait for clients to tell us which ones — every claim with retention withheld and an invoice raised gross is a query we can run against our own data today.

What we do about each one

Two states, two different conversations. Neither of them starts with the client noticing.
1
Run the query and own the number. Before anyone is contacted we should know exactly how many claims, across how many tenants, and for how much. That figure decides whether this is a quiet fix or a disclosure.
2
Unpaid invoices — reissue at the corrected amount. The client has not moved money, so this is a corrected document and a sentence of explanation.
3
Paid invoices — the contractor has been overpaid and the retention was never withheld in cash. Credit note, or an adjustment on the next claim. Which of the two is the contractor's call, not ours — it changes their cash position and their client's.
4
Tell the beta group before they find it. The claim→invoice→Xero beta drafts already disclose this defect. Anyone already invoicing out of Varicon on a retention contract needs the same notice, and it should come from us.
Needs a decision Does the correction reach Xero automatically? These invoices are synced. A credit note raised in Varicon that does not flow through leaves the two systems disagreeing, which is the same class of defect one layer along. Not scoped here.
