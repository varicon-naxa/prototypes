The Employee Upload Template as it ships today

Read from Employee_Upload_Template.xlsx, downloaded 31 Aug 2026. Ticket import is not missing — it is already here, and it stops one field short of being useful.

Twelve sheets. One you fill in (EMPLOYEES), six hidden lookup sheets feeding dropdowns (_EmploymentType, _Allowances, _Role, _Status, _CompanyName, _Tickets), and five visible sheets that reprint the same lists again — one of which, Pay Category, is a header and nothing else.

Four things wrong with column M

1One dropdown cell means one ticket per employee. A worker holding a white card, a first aid certificate and an HR licence cannot be expressed. Most workers hold three or four.
2It carries the type and nothing else. No number, no issued date, no expiry, no issuing body, no state. So every imported ticket is a draft shell somebody opens and completes by hand — the importer moves the drawer-by-drawer work, it does not remove it. And with no expiry date, the register still cannot tell you what lapses next month, which is the entire reason to hold the data.
3The workbook already has the idiom and tickets did not get it. Allowances and Form Numbers both say "separated by a comma" and take many values in one cell. Tickets — the column with the dropdown — takes one. Even comma-separation would not be enough here, because a ticket has dates, but the inconsistency shows the column was an afterthought.
4There is no download of your existing employees. The file ships with three dummy rows — James Baker VAR-1, Ford Humphries VAR-2, Matilda Grace Anderson VAR-3 — and blank Company and Role. It is a create-only form. Nothing round-trips, so there is no way to correct at scale, and no protection against a second upload duplicating everyone.

What changes

Not a new importer. The same workbook, three edits:

  1. A locked Record ID column in front of the EMPLOYEES sheet, present only on an export of your real employees. It is what makes a re-upload update instead of duplicate.
  2. A second sheet, TICKETS — one row per ticket, keyed on Employee ID, carrying Ticket Type, Number, Issued, Expiry, Issuing Body, State, Notes, and a read-only Document column. Its own locked Ticket Record ID for the same reason.
  3. Column M stays and keeps working, so nothing breaks for anyone using it. Its comment now says what it actually does and points at the TICKETS sheet. If an employee appears on TICKETS, column M is ignored for that employee.

The built file is real — not a mockup. Download the revised workbook: it is the production template with those edits applied, dropdowns repointed after the column insert, validations widened to 500 rows, a hidden _State lookup added, the empty Pay Category sheet dropped, and every new column carrying a Varicon-authored cell comment in the same voice as the existing ones.

Two things in the file worth a separate look. The _Role dropdown carries milan, SagarTestResource and Supervisor 1 — test data in a customer-facing list (this may just be the tenant this copy was exported from — unverified). And _Tickets names its eight types inconsistently: five carry a code, "Working at Heights" and "Driving Licence" do not. Ticket types are about to be matched on their name, so that list wants a tidy first.

What are you uploading?

Two different things share the word "ticket". Say which one up front and every later step changes to match.
Worker tickets
The licences and certificates your people hold — a row per worker per ticket. This is the register on the Tickets page.
Ticket types (the catalogue)
The list of tickets that exist in your tenant — code, name, category, expiry rule, whether a document is required. Same wizard, feeds Manage Ticket Types.
Do this first if your ticket types aren't set up yet — worker tickets are matched against it.

What should the template contain?

This is the same Employee Upload Template you download today — one extra sheet, and the option to have it come down already filled with your people. The choice below is what decides whether a returned row adds or corrects: a pre-filled export carries a locked Record ID, a blank one does not.
Pre-filled with your employees — TICKETS sheet empty
Every active employee already on the EMPLOYEES sheet, locked, with their Record ID. You add rows to the TICKETS sheet against their Employee ID. Nobody has to spell a name the way we spell it.
Best for the first load, or a course a whole crew just sat.
Pre-filled with your employees and their current tickets
Everything on file today on both sheets. Change an expiry, add a number, fix a typo, re-upload — each row carries its Ticket Record ID, so it updates rather than duplicating.
Best for a renewal run or a data clean-up. This is the download-and-reupload path.
Blank template — headers only
What you get today, plus the TICKETS sheet. For pasting from a subbie's spreadsheet or a training provider's report. Employees are matched on Employee ID, then email, then exact name within company.
No Record IDs, so every row creates. Anything ambiguous is held back, never guessed.

What the prototype commits to

Nine rules. Each one is a decision someone has to make before this gets built, and each is made here rather than left to the implementation.

  1. Add from the list page is the existing form with a Worker field on the front, in a side panel. Ticket type, number, issued, expiry, issuing body, state, document, notes, and the Save as draft / Publish pair are the worker-drawer form verbatim. One component, two entry points — inline on the worker's Tickets tab where the worker is already known, in the standard right-hand panel from the list page where it isn't. Two forms would drift within a release.
  2. One worker per ticket. A ticket is a record about a person — its number, its document and its expiry all belong to that person — so the form writes exactly one. When a crew of six sits the same course on the same day, that is six rows, and keying them one at a time through this panel is the wrong tool. It is the case bulk upload exists for, which is why the two buttons sit side by side rather than one being a power-user afterthought.
  3. A pre-filled export is the update path; a blank template is the create path. The difference is a locked Record ID column that only pre-filled exports carry. A row with an ID updates that record. A row without one creates. Nothing is ever matched on a person's name alone to decide between the two.
  4. Worker matching on a blank template is employee ID, then email, then exact name within company. Two candidates is an error row, not a best guess. Getting a licence onto the wrong person is worse than not importing it.
  5. A ticket type that isn't in the catalogue never appears silently. The review step lists the unknown types and offers one checkbox to add them to Manage Ticket Types. Left unchecked, those rows are errors. Free-text ticket types are how a register becomes ninety spellings of "white card".
  6. Blank expiry is derived from the ticket type's rule; a past expiry imports as Expired. An expired licence is not a bad row — it's the reason you're loading the data. Rejecting it hides the risk you bought the module for.
  7. Documents can't come through a spreadsheet. Where the ticket type requires one, imported rows land as Draft — document missing, in the Draft tab that already exists, and are chased from there. Consistent with the standing rule that compliance gates flag rather than block.
  8. The review step is a dry run and the only way to import. Counted as New / Update / Warning / Error, filterable, fixable inline. Skipping the error rows imports the rest. There is no path that writes on upload.
  9. Column M keeps working. The existing Tickets dropdown still creates a draft shell, so nobody's saved workbook breaks. Where an employee appears on the TICKETS sheet, column M is ignored for that employee — the richer source wins, and the rule is stated rather than discovered.
  10. Every import is one reversible batch. The Activity tab records who imported what, when, from which file, with a single undo while the batch is the most recent change to those records.

Statuses, since the tiles depend on them

Valid
Has an expiry date, more than 30 days out.
Expiring soon
Expiry within 30 days. Threshold is a tenant setting, not a constant.
Expired
Expiry in the past.
No expiry
The ticket type carries no expiry rule. Counts as valid, and is why the current page shows 1 total against 0 + 0 + 0.
Draft
Incomplete — missing a required document or number. Not counted in compliance either way.

Open questions

Three things the prototype deliberately shows rather than answers.

  • Answered by the file, not by us: there is one importer, and it is the Employee Upload Template behind Initial Data Upload. It already has a Tickets column. So this is not a new importer — it is that workbook plus a TICKETS sheet and a Record ID, reached by a second door on this page. See Template today. What is still open is whether the Tickets page's door and the Initial Data Upload door share one wizard, or whether the tickets door is a thin scoped version of it.
  • Who loads a subcontractor's tickets? Today the head contractor keys the subbie's crew. The export/re-upload pair is exactly the shape you'd hand a subbie to fill in themselves — which is a different permission model and probably a different release.
  • Does a ticket expiring stop anything? The register only pays for itself when an expired high-risk licence is visible where the work is assigned — the scheduler, the SWMS, the site sign-in. Flag, don't block, per the standing decision. Out of scope here, but it's the reason this page matters.
