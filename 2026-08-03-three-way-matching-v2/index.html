Overview

Three documents, one question: should we pay this?

A supplier bill is a claim. On its own it proves nothing — it is the supplier's assertion about what they sent and what it costs. Three-way matching answers the claim with two independent records that Varicon already holds: what we authorised, and what the site crew actually received. When all three agree, the bill is safe to pay. When they disagree, the disagreement itself is the useful output.

◆ Why the direction matters

The match is not a three-way average — it is a one-way test. Rate comes from the PO, quantity comes from the docket, and the bill is checked against the product of those two. Getting this backwards (letting the bill set the rate because it "looks close") is what makes matching theatre instead of control.

The rule, stated once

Expected value of a bill line
PO unit rate × Σ docket quantities = Expected amount
Every flag in the engine is a comparison against that one number. price variance is billed amount minus expected amount. qty variance is billed quantity minus summed docket quantity. off-PO means no PO line to take a rate from. missing-docket means no docket quantity to multiply by. There is no fifth kind of problem.

Where this sits in the build

The engine contract

VDP-139 already specifies the output shape. This prototype is built to it rather than inventing a parallel model — per bill line the engine returns:

matched_po_line
which PO line supplied the rate
matched_dockets
which dockets supplied the quantity
expected_qty
sum of docket quantities
expected_price
PO rate × expected qty
variance_flags
qty · price · off-PO · missing-docket

Aggregated to a bill-level match_status of matched partial unmatched.

What is genuinely undecided

No tolerance value is documented anywhere in the wiki — not a percentage, not a dollar cap, not a rounding allowance. VDP-139 says thresholds are "configurable per tenant" and adds the instruction: "get the variance threshold defaults reviewed by finance before implementing."

That review has not happened. So the numbers in the Sandbox are a proposal to argue with, not a spec to build. Turning the knobs and watching which bills flip status is the fastest way to have that argument.

◆ What the match takes from here

Only two things: the unit rate per line, and the order quantity as a ceiling for over-receipt checks. Cost centre and accounting code ride along as coding defaults — though see the open question below on whether that default should come from the PO at all.

▲ Open decision · cost centre source of truth

CQ-2680 and AKJ Services (Pylon #13061) both ask that cost centre default from the docket, not the PO — because the crew on site knows which cost centre the concrete actually went into, and a single PO often feeds several. No decision has been recorded either way. This prototype defaults from the docket where a docket exists, and falls back to the PO where one doesn't. It is arguably a prerequisite for VDP-134, not a follow-up.

✖ Live bug that corrupts this leg

PICBR-912 — editing a delivery docket's received quantity silently overwrites the PO's order quantity. If the authority leg can be rewritten by the leg it is supposed to check, the match proves nothing. This has to be fixed before the engine can be trusted; it was last seen Paused at High priority.

What the crew is actually doing

Photographing the docket takes five seconds; OCR fills supplier, quantity, units, date and ticket reference. The part that needs a human is splitting the load across cost centres — 8 m³ turned up on one truck but 5 went into the slab and 3 into the access road. Nobody in the office can reconstruct that later.

Two things worth noticing

Σ
One PO line can gather quantity from many dockets
A 40 m³ PO line might be filled by five pours over three weeks. expected_qty is the sum across every docket linked to that line — which is why a bill covering three dockets still matches cleanly.
＋
Dockets can carry items that aren't on the PO
The wash-out fee is real, was incurred, and was never ordered. Capturing it here is what lets an off-PO bill line auto-match later instead of landing on the AP officer as a mystery charge. It needs its own cost centre, since there's no PO line to inherit from.
◆ Over-receipt is a warning, not a block

The only over-delivery control that exists today is a soft prompt when delivered qty exceeds ordered qty (PICBR-1577). Deliberate: over-delivery is usually legitimate on civil jobs, and blocking the docket would just push the crew to record nothing. The check belongs at bill approval, where someone can accept it — not on a phone in a concrete truck.

✖ Live bug · unapproved POs are linkable

PICBR-1565 — dockets on POs that haven't cleared approval stay selectable, so a bill can be matched against unauthorised spend. CQ-2709 asks for this to be blocked at docket creation.

Step 3 of 7 · The claim

The bill lands and Varicon works out what it relates to

Before anything can be compared, the bill has to be attached to the right PO. This is inference, not matching — and it is allowed to fail. VDP-140 is explicit that uncertain inference returns candidates rather than guessing, which is the correct instinct: a confidently wrong PO link is worse than an unresolved one.

Do not trust OCR output as a match input without a second leg
OCR on bills already misbehaves in production in ways that would poison a match: VDP-1334 fabricates a line item that isn't on the invoice (Moore Civil), VDP-234 assigns the wrong supplier entirely, and VDP-694 reads credit notes as invoices. This is precisely the argument for three-way matching: the docket and PO are independent of the OCR, so an invented bill line lands as off-PO with no docket behind it — visible, rather than silently paid.
▲ Open decision · already-billed dockets

The single largest double-pay exposure. VDP-134's acceptance criteria say the linking modal should "filter out dockets already billed in earlier invoices by default" — but "by default" implies the filter can be turned off, and nothing specifies what happens to a partially billed docket. If 8 m³ was received and only 5 m³ billed last month, does the docket reappear with 3 m³ available? The engine needs a per-docket-line consumed quantity, which VDP-141's schema does not currently carry.

◆ Design note · this replaces the v1 Add Bill table

v1 reused the production Add Bill layout, which puts thirteen editable columns in front of the user and hides the comparison inside two of them ("Docket qty" and "PO unit cost"). The derivation is the point of the screen, so in v2 it gets its own read-only table and the editable coding grid comes after. VDP-137 says "match the prototype's layout closely" — it should be pointed at this one.

Step 6 of 7 · The decision points

When the legs disagree

A flagged line is not an error — it is a question that needs an owner. The engine's job ends at "these numbers differ by this much"; deciding what to do is a human act with money attached. What matters for the build is that each flag has a bounded set of resolutions, and that whichever one is chosen gets recorded.

▲ Open decision · does a missing docket block payment?

This is the biggest unanswered policy question in the feature, and it is a business decision, not a technical one. Today nothing prevents billing against a PO with no docket — PICBR-1495 shows un-docketed bill lines not only exist but silently drop out of Daily Cost Tracking, which is the worst of both worlds: paid and invisible.

Three positions, and the prototype can demonstrate any of them:

  • Hard block — maximum control, but AP stalls whenever the crew is slow, and pressure to fabricate dockets appears. Not recommended.
  • Soft warning + attestation — approver explicitly confirms receipt, name recorded. Keeps the audit trail honest without stopping the business. Recommended default.
  • Per-tenant setting — likely where this lands, given every other threshold in the feature is tenant-configurable. Costs a settings surface and a migration decision on the default.

The ambiguity case, which has no clean answer

?
Two PO lines share a rate — which one does this bill line belong to?
If 32 MPa and 40 MPa concrete are both priced at $220/m³ and the bill says only "concrete supply", matching on rate is genuinely ambiguous. The engine must not pick one silently: the two lines have different cost centres and different remaining order quantities, so a coin-flip misallocates cost. Return both candidates and make the user choose — the same instinct VDP-140 applies to PO inference.

Proposed matching precedence

Bill line → PO line, in order. First unambiguous hit wins; ties escalate to the user.

1
Explicit PO line reference on the bill (rare, but decisive)
2
Resource / description exact match
3
Description fuzzy match + rate match
4
Rate match alone — only if unique across open PO lines
5
Otherwise: return candidates, flag ambiguous

Not in VDP-139 — it specifies the output shape but not how a bill line finds its PO line. Worth adding before implementation.

Rate variance is asymmetric

Billed above the PO rate is a commercial problem — someone is being charged more than was agreed. Billed below is not; it is a discount, and blocking it wastes everyone's time.

So the tolerance should almost certainly be one-sided: flag overs, pass unders. The Sandbox has a toggle for this because it is a real choice — symmetric tolerance is defensible if you care about data quality and want to know when a supplier's pricing drifts either way.

Also relevant: supplier rates are truncated at 2 decimal places internally, causing per-cent discrepancies on large quantities (PICBR-1184). A rounding allowance isn't optional — without one, a 10,000-unit line fails on arithmetic noise.

Three downstream consumers

📊
Daily Cost Tracking

Approved bill lines land on their cost centre and drive cost-to-date, budget variance and forecast. The cost-centre split from the docket is what makes this accurate — 5 m³ to the slab, 3 m³ to the access road.

PICBR-1495: bill lines not linked to a docket are currently not tracked here at all. Matching is what closes that hole.

⇄
Xero / MYOB

On reaching Awaiting Payment the bill pushes out. Sync trigger is a tenant setting; recommended config is after the final approver, so nothing unmatched leaves Varicon.

Match status is a sibling of sync status, and VDP-138 flags surfacing both on one list as an open UX call. AP officers already can't filter on sync status (VDP-90) — don't repeat that.

📋
The PO itself

Billed quantity accumulates against each PO line, driving the fully-billed prompt and the short-close path. A PO can only be closed confidently if its billed quantities are trustworthy.

The PO summary's "Docket Delivered" amount is the closest thing today to a received-vs-ordered rollup — and it doesn't update when a linked docket is created (PICBR-1321).

✖ No path back once matched and approved

A rejected bill currently has no legal route back to Awaiting Approval even after the reject reason is fixed — the workaround is delete-and-recreate (PICBR-1750). Add a match to that and it gets worse: re-matching after a data correction is explicitly out of scope for VDP-134, so if a docket is fixed after the bill was matched, the stored match result is stale and nothing recomputes it. VDP-141 says match status is "recomputed on edit" — but an edit to the docket is not an edit to the bill. That gap needs closing.

Step 5 of 7 · The consumption check

Previously billed — why this is really a four-way check

A PO and a docket are not one-shot documents. They are budgets that get consumed, often by many bills over weeks. So the question is never "does this bill match the PO and dockets?" — it is "does this bill fit in what's left of them?" Without the prior-bills leg, three bills of 8 m³ each against one 8 m³ docket all match perfectly and you pay three times.

$2,600 / $5,000 against the PO is fine — there is headroom. $2,600 / $2,500 against the docket is not: this bill claims $100 more than has been evidenced as received, once prior bills are counted. The PO says we could spend it; the dockets say it hasn't arrived. The tighter of the two ceilings governs.

Consume dockets, not just PO lines

The modelling decision that matters

Consumption has to be tracked at docket-line granularity, not just as a total per PO line. Otherwise you cannot answer "which of these dockets is still available?" — which is exactly what VDP-134's linking modal needs in order to filter already-billed dockets out.

And it must support partial consumption. An 8 m³ docket with 5 m³ billed should reappear offering 3 m³, not vanish and not reappear whole. VDP-141's proposed schema stores matched_docket_ids[] — a flat list, which cannot express "3 of 8 remaining". That needs to become a through-model carrying a quantity.

States a docket line can be in

Part-billed

Over-billed

▲ Open decision · do draft bills reserve docket quantity?

Two AP officers open two draft bills against the same docket at the same time. Both see 8 m³ available; both claim it; both get approved. Nothing in VDP-141 prevents this.

Option A — consume on approval only. Simple, and matches how the rest of Varicon behaves, but allows the race. Needs a re-validation at the approval boundary that can fail an already-approved-by-tier-1 bill, which is a confusing user experience.

Option B — soft reservation on draft. Draft claims mark quantity reserved and show "3 m³ available (5 m³ on draft bill INV-…)". More honest, and cheap in Django — but requires reservation release on draft delete, and a staleness sweep for abandoned drafts.

Recommendation: B for visibility, A for enforcement — reservations are advisory in the UI, and the authoritative consumption check runs inside the approval transaction. That way the race is impossible without drafts being able to permanently lock quantity.

✖ Credit notes invert the ledger and are unmodelled

A credit note reduces previously-billed quantity, so it must return consumption to the pool. Nothing in VDP-134 or VDP-139 mentions credit notes, and OCR currently misreads them as invoices (VDP-694) — which would add consumption instead of removing it, then permanently block the re-bill. Partial credit allocation is already requested (CQ-2696). Signed quantities in the consumption ledger handle this cleanly; bolting it on later will not.
